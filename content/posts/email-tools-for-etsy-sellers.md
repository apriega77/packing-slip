Etsy brings you buyers, but it keeps the relationship. An email list is the one audience a shop owns outright. This guide compares the entry-level plans of three email tools and starts with a rule that is easy to miss.

## Before you pick a tool: the consent rule

You cannot copy buyer email addresses from your Etsy orders into a mailing list. Etsy's guidance for sellers says you need a buyer's express consent before you send them marketing messages, even when you received the address through Etsy to fulfil an order. Consent means a clear action by the buyer, such as filling in your signup form themselves. They can also withdraw it at any time, so every email needs a working unsubscribe link.

All three tools below give you a signup form and add the unsubscribe link for you. Read Etsy's Seller Policy yourself before you start, because it is the source that counts.

## Systeme.io

[Systeme.io](aff:systeme) has the roomiest free plan of the three: 2,000 contacts and unlimited sends, with no card needed and no expiry date. The catch is in the automation. The free plan allows one automation rule and one email campaign, so you can set up one automated sequence, such as a welcome series, but not much more. It is also a wider tool than the other two, with sales funnels, a blog and course hosting that an Etsy seller may never open.

It suits a shop that wants to stay free for as long as possible and only needs a signup form, a welcome email and a regular newsletter.

## MailerLite

[MailerLite](aff:mailerlite) is the most focused on newsletters. Its free plan covers up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, which works out to ten emails per subscriber if your list is full. Go past 250 subscribers on the Free plan and MailerLite holds off sending further emails until you upgrade or reduce your subscriber count. Paid Comfort plans start from $12 a month on monthly billing at 500 subscribers ($10.80 a month billed yearly).

It suits a shop that expects a small list for a while, or one that wants a tool built mainly for newsletters.

## AWeber

[AWeber](aff:aweber) offers a free plan, though its main pricing table only lists paid plans. On [aweber.com/pricing.htm](https://www.aweber.com/pricing.htm), the comparison table leads with a 14-day trial across three paid tiers, starting with Lite at $15 a month on monthly billing ($12.49 a month billed annually) for up to 500 subscribers. On that same page, a "Get started for free" section and header link point to a separate free-plan page ([aweber.com/free.htm](https://www.aweber.com/free.htm)) that covers up to 500 subscribers and 3,000 emails a month with no time limit and no credit card required, though feature-by-feature limits are not tabulated there.

It suits a seller who wants to start free with up to 500 subscribers or prefers a paid plan from the start without planning around a 250-subscriber free cap.

## Which one to start with

- If you have no list yet and no budget, start with Systeme.io's free plan.
- If you want the simplest newsletter tool and your list is under 250 people, MailerLite's free plan is enough.
- If you plan to pay anyway, compare MailerLite and AWeber at your expected list size, because both charge more as the list grows.

Whichever you choose, set up the signup form first and write one welcome email.
